# 07: Beauty Businesses

**Tier 2, strong potential.** Premium salons, hair studios, nail and beauty concepts, med-spas, high-end independent operators.

See [00-overview](00-overview-market-context.md) for the shared 2026 context.

---

## 1. Market right now
- **Digital booking is the norm.** ~72% of UAE salons use a digital booking system and 82%+ of online bookings are on mobile.
- **Marketplace cost pressure.** Fresha is dominant, but its **commission on new marketplace clients** (reported ~20%) is eating into margins. Salons want direct booking.
- **Tech expectations are rising.** ~40% of top-tier Dubai salons reportedly offer AI colour previews. 74% of 25–34s say they'd switch salons for AI skin analysis and personalised treatments.
- **90% of MENA beauty purchases** are influenced by Instagram and TikTok content.
- **Service trends:** scalp health is going mainstream (68% of under-40s rate it a priority). **Men's grooming is moving upmarket**, with average male spend per visit up from AED 120 to AED 340 since 2023.
- **Med-spas** fall under DHA/DoH advertising rules (see [03-premium-clinics](03-premium-clinics.md)).

## 2. What their customers are choosing
- **The stylist or technician, not the salon.** Loyalty follows people.
- **Specialisation** (colour specialist, scalp clinic, brow studio) over "we do everything."
- **Personalised, tech-enabled consultations.**
- **Visible proof**: transformations, reels of process, reviews.
- **Easy booking** through Instagram, WhatsApp and an app.

## 3. What the owner is thinking
- "Top stylists leave and take their clients with them."
- "Marketplace commissions are killing our margin on new clients."
- "Every salon posts the same balayage reel."
- "Filling Sunday to Tuesday is hard."

## 4. Main pain points
1. **Stylist-dependent revenue.**
2. **Marketplace dependency and commission.**
3. **Undifferentiated content.**
4. **Rebooking and retention.**
5. **Off-peak demand.**
6. **Rising client acquisition costs.**

## 5. What works in marketing now
| Lever | How |
|---|---|
| **Stylist-led series under the salon brand** | Each specialist has a format, but the brand owns the account |
| **Transformation and process reels** | Real results, honest lighting (edited-image rules apply to med-spas) |
| **Specialist positioning** | "The scalp health studio," "The colour correction experts" |
| **Direct booking + WhatsApp** | Move repeat clients off marketplaces onto owned booking |
| **Rebooking automation** | Reminder cycles by service (e.g. colour every 6–8 weeks) |
| **Men's grooming content** | A growing, under-served segment |
| **Local micro creators** | Regular clients who create, permit-checked |

## 6. What doesn't work
- Generic "book now" promo graphics.
- Discount blasts that attract bargain-hunters who never rebook.
- Letting stylists run the only strong accounts.

## 7. Example scenario (illustrative)
**A premium hair studio in Jumeirah with 8 stylists. 40% of new clients come from marketplaces, the brand account is weak, and two stylists have strong personal followings.**
- **Fix:**
  1. Monthly shoot of stylist series on the *brand* account (with the stylists collaborating).
  2. Specialist positioning around colour.
  3. Direct booking link and WhatsApp everywhere.
  4. Automated rebooking.
  5. A men's grooming line with its own content.
- **Measure:** share of bookings that are direct, rebooking rate, revenue per client, off-peak fill.

## 8. Best offer for this segment (*Offer*)
**"Chair Filler" retainer:** monthly content, direct-booking and WhatsApp setup, rebooking automation, and specialist positioning.

## 9. KPIs to report
Direct vs. marketplace booking share, new-client cost, rebooking rate, client lifetime value, off-peak utilisation.

## 10. Outreach hook
> "Every new client from the marketplace costs you a commission. Here's how to turn your stylists' content into direct bookings the salon owns."
